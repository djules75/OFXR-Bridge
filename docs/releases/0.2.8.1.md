# OFXR Bridge v0.2.8.1 (V371)

An unofficial build on
[tig3rmast3r's v0.2.1](https://github.com/tig3rmast3r/OFXR-Bridge/releases).
A frame-pacing release after [0.2.7.3](0.2.7.3.md), for headsets on Pimax's
own OpenXR runtime and on Virtual Desktop. SteamVR is unchanged.

## What's new

- **Smoother on Pimax headsets using Pimax's own OpenXR runtime.** The
  bridge and the Pimax software were each trying to decide when frames went
  to the headset, and they got in each other's way. Several times a second
  the headset received two frames at once and then none, which you saw as a
  small repeat followed by a jump. The bridge now lets Pimax set the timing.
  In our tests the repeats went from about 6 in every 100 frames to almost
  none.
- **More even motion on Pimax and Virtual Desktop.** Even when no frames
  were lost, about a third of the game's frames were shown slightly too
  early or too late, so motion never felt fully even. The bridge now shows
  the game's frames at a steady rhythm, so turning your head and flying feel
  smoother. This adds a few thousandths of a second of delay, which you
  shouldn't notice.
- **Fewer hitches on Virtual Desktop when the game can't keep up.** With the
  refresh rate set higher than the game can handle, for example 144 Hz with
  a game running at 55 fps, the bridge sometimes sent two frames almost
  together and the headset showed only one of them. That no longer happens.

The synthesis itself, the tray and the settings are unchanged.

## Known issues

- **Pick a refresh rate close to double your game's frame rate.** Nothing
  in this release changes that rule. At 144 Hz with a game that makes 55 fps,
  a quarter of the refreshes show a repeat with every version of the bridge;
  at 90 Hz the same game and scene run clean. The FPS number shows when
  this is happening.
- **Pimax: jitter on head movement that is fine when looking straight is
  not the bridge.** If it is there with the bridge disarmed too, it is the
  headset's tracking map. Quit Pimax Play, rename
  `C:\ProgramData\Pimax\slam\<serial>platMap_orborb_K8L4.bin` and the `.yaml`
  beside it to `.old`, restart Pimax Play and look around the room for a
  minute so it builds a fresh map. Do not touch `device_calibration_*.xml`.
- **SteamVR: turn off Motion Smoothing and "fixed frame rate at half" for
  the game** in SteamVR's per-application video settings. When a game's GPU
  time reaches the refresh budget, SteamVR starts halving its frame rate on
  and off, and the bridge's pairs land late: the result is stutter and a
  performance drop that looks like a bridge regression. Check this first.
- **No FPS overlay in Vulkan games.** Vulkan games have no reliable FPS
  reading yet.
- **Dips are often a resolution ceiling, not the bridge.** Disarm the bridge
  and play the same scene: if the dips remain, lower the per-eye resolution.

## Installing

Unzip anywhere and run `OFXRBridgeTray.exe`. It arms the bridge by itself.
Keep `OFXRBridgeTray.exe` and the `ofxr` folder together; it holds the OpenXR
layer, its settings and the Vulkan layer. If an earlier version is armed,
disarm it and close its tray first.

## Tested

With a **Pimax Crystal Super** on an RTX 5090, on Pimax OpenXR (72 and
90 Hz) and SteamVR (72 Hz), and a **Quest 3** through Virtual Desktop (90,
120 and 144 Hz), all in MSFS 2024.

Please report results, working or not, on this fork's
[GitHub Issues](../../issues), with an OFXR flight log where you can. To
record one, enable **Bridge flight recorder** in the tray, reproduce the
problem, then use **Open bridge logs**. Logs are written under
`%LOCALAPPDATA%\OFXR Bridge\RuntimeLayer\v371\`.
